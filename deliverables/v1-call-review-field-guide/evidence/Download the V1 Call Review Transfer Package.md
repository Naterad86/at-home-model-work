# V1 Transfer Package: Coaching Partner Call-Review Workflow

## Purpose and Scope

This package captures the **actual current-state workflow** used by a Coaching Partner to select, review, document, deliver, and follow up on call-review coaching.

It is intended for continued development of a V1 Coaching Partner Field Guide. It preserves workflow structure while excluding customer identities, agent-specific performance information, proprietary call content, and other unnecessary enterprise payload.

### Evidence labels

- **Observed practice:** Described as part of the actual workflow.
- **Guidance-supported:** Appears in supplied coaching resources, but may not be universally prescribed.
- **Personal method:** A technique or workaround used by a Coaching Partner.
- **Inference:** Reasonably suggested by the evidence but not verified.
- **Unknown:** Requires further investigation.

---

# 1. Current-State Call-Review Workflow

## Stage 1: Recognize the Review Need

The call-review workflow begins before a call is opened. It starts when a business need, performance signal, or review obligation is identified.

### Common triggers

- Direct leadership request based on a previously identified:
  - performance signal;
  - coaching need;
  - compliance concern;
  - agent category or qualifying condition.
- New-hire call-review expectations:
  - minimum target of three reviewed calls per month;
  - five reviews preferred.
- A behavior currently emphasized for the new-hire class.
- Agent KPI or production results indicating a likely development need.
- Intraday performance monitoring by the CP.
- Performance patterns observed across broader timeframes.
- CP judgment.
- Agent request.
- A specific interaction identified by leadership.

### Scope

Leadership may direct the CP to review:

- the full class;
- a subset of agents meeting a specified condition;
- one agent;
- one specific interaction.

### Classification

- **Observed practice:** Leadership requests are the most common trigger.
- **Observed practice:** CPs monitor numbers both intraday and over time.
- **Not yet verified:** Whether the three-call minimum and five-call preference are formally documented requirements or established operating expectations.

---

## Stage 2: Establish the Review Focus

The review need is translated into a behavior or concern that will guide the work.

Possible sources of the focal behavior include:

- current class behavior focus;
- KPI or production evidence;
- prior coaching;
- compliance concern;
- leadership direction;
- CP judgment.

### Operating rule

Each individual call review should have **one principal behavior focus**.

The CP may consider multiple possible behaviors during preparation or synthesis, but the individual review remains centered on one behavior.

### Examples of behavior-centered analysis

A review focused on asking for the sale may consider:

- whether sufficient rapport was established;
- whether the presentation created a natural transition;
- whether the agent used an effective closing statement;
- what occurred when the opportunity to ask arose.

Supporting actions are included when they explain the focal behavior. They are not treated as separate coaching topics unless needed later.

---

## Stage 3: Gather Preliminary Context

Before selecting or analyzing the call, the CP may gather contextual evidence.

### Potential context

- previous coaching;
- KPI performance;
- production results;
- current class focus;
- leadership concerns;
- contemporaneous communication;
- previous QA or call-review findings.

The amount of context gathered varies according to the review purpose.

### Important boundary

Context should help guide the review without causing the CP to predetermine the call result. Individual calls remain evidence to be evaluated rather than examples selected solely to confirm an existing conclusion.

---

## Stage 4: Choose the Call-Retrieval Environment

### Primary environment: AWS Leader View

AWS Leader View is the preferred environment for:

- searching department interactions;
- narrowing the interaction population;
- selecting individual calls;
- listening to calls;
- using playback controls.

AWS provides the most practical call-selection interface.

### Secondary environment: Verint

Verint can also be used to retrieve and review calls. It offers additional evidence, including:

- screen recording;
- visibility into QA review structure;
- past reviews received by the agent.

Verint is generally more tedious than AWS. It is used when the additional visual, QA, or historical evidence justifies the effort.

### Selection decision

The CP decides whether:

- AWS audio and interaction information are sufficient; or
- screen activity, QA context, or prior review history makes Verint necessary.

---

## Stage 5: Define the Eligible Call Population

The CP narrows the available interactions before choosing a call.

### Date range

- The date range should be relevant to the review scope.
- Time since the agent’s previous coaching may influence the range.
- Calls are ideally no more than one or two weeks old.

### Call direction

- Inbound interactions are normally reviewed.
- Outbound interactions are generally excluded unless the circumstances require them.

### Queue or lead category

Calls are distributed through different queue or lead categories. These categories help the CP understand:

- how the interaction originated;
- the likely context of the customer journey;
- whether the call was inbound or outbound;
- whether the interaction is relevant to the target behavior.

### Call length and scorability

The call generally needs to be long enough to contain meaningful evidence, but there is no stable universal minimum.

- Longer calls may offer more evidence.
- Short calls may still illustrate a behavior or compliance concern.
- “Scorable” is therefore a judgment-based threshold rather than a fixed duration.

### Purpose-based exclusions

Interactions that cannot reasonably contain the target opportunity may be excluded.

For example, when reviewing a product-related behavior, the CP may remove interactions involving a product that the agent is not authorized or trained to sell.

Browser search, such as **Ctrl+F**, may be used to filter the visible interaction list quickly.

### Leadership-specified interaction

A specific call identified by leadership can override the normal sampling process.

---

## Stage 6: Select the Call

After legitimate eligibility filters are applied, **random sampling is the preferred and fairest selection method**.

This is important because the following information is generally not visible before review:

- sales result;
- interaction outcome;
- confirmed behavior execution;
- substantial behavioral signals.

AWS may show limited interaction facts, such as which party disconnected, but meaningful behavioral evidence normally requires listening to the call or reviewing a transcript.

### Selection principle

The goal is not purely random selection from every interaction. It is random selection from a **legitimately eligible population** defined by:

- scope;
- recency;
- call direction;
- queue category;
- behavioral opportunity;
- practical scorability;
- necessary exclusions.

---

## Stage 7: Review the Call

The CP listens to the broader interaction while directing attention toward the selected behavior.

### Review actions

The CP may:

- listen to the full interaction or relevant portions;
- pause;
- rewind;
- change playback speed;
- replay key moments;
- verify wording or sequence;
- note the surrounding conditions that affected the behavior.

### Review philosophy

The review does not attempt to replace human interpretation with narrow scoring. It directs human attention toward the most relevant evidence while preserving enough broader context to interpret the behavior accurately.

### Knowledge resource

The New Hire Guide is the primary agent-facing job aid for:

- systems navigation;
- troubleshooting;
- expected processes;
- resource-driven pathways.

The New Hire Guide can help determine whether an expected operational pathway was followed. It is not itself a coaching methodology or scorecard.

---

## Stage 8: Capture Working Evidence in OneNote

OneNote is the CP’s repository for working notes.

Potential content includes:

- individual-call observations;
- timestamps or key moments;
- surrounding call context;
- secondary findings;
- provisional interpretations;
- questions to explore with the agent;
- material for the eventual cross-call summary.

### Information separation

The CP separates:

1. evidence directly related to the focal behavior;
2. broader context needed to interpret that behavior;
3. additional findings that may not need immediate coaching.

Secondary findings may be:

- included as additional comments;
- retained in OneNote;
- revisited later if a pattern develops.

### Classification

- **Observed practice:** OneNote is the repository for CP notes.
- **Unknown:** Whether OneNote is an official requirement or the chosen local method.

---

## Stage 9: Form a Provisional Call-Level Finding

An individual call can establish that a gap or missed opportunity occurred. It does not necessarily establish the complete root cause or broader pattern.

### Current diagnostic posture

Agents have generally already been exposed to:

- system-navigation training;
- expected processes;
- talk paths;
- behavioral workshops.

Regression commonly appears as agents become more comfortable. For that reason, observed gaps are often initially treated as inconsistent execution rather than lack of prior exposure.

However, prior exposure does not prove the cause of the current failure.

### Provisional conclusion

The CP preserves the call-level conclusion as **potential** until all selected calls and relevant materials are reviewed together.

The workflow distinguishes between:

- the observable instance;
- a possible explanation;
- the broader pattern;
- the eventual coaching conclusion.

---

## Stage 10: Create the Individual PHUB Coaching Entry

Performance Hub functions as the practical call-review scorecard and formal coaching record.

### Standardized content

The PHUB entry includes:

1. feedback framed around the focal behavior;
2. additional comments;
3. the agent or leader commitment, when applicable;
4. the follow-up timeframe, when applicable.

### Documentation principle

The entry should:

- identify execution of the focal behavior;
- provide feedback on improvement;
- avoid transcribing the entire call;
- avoid diluting the review with unrelated behaviors;
- set expectations for what happens next.

Each reviewed call receives its own behavior-focused PHUB entry.

---

## Stage 11: Repeat the Review for Additional Calls

When multiple calls are included in the assignment, the CP repeats the selection, review, evidence capture, and PHUB documentation process.

Each call remains an individual evidence unit.

### Example communication structure

If three calls are reviewed:

- three individual PHUB coaching records are produced;
- each individual review may be communicated separately;
- a fourth communication contains the cross-call summary analysis.

The relationship between PHUB’s automatic distribution and the separate emails requires further clarification.

---

## Stage 12: Synthesize the Complete Evidence Set

The CP performs a summary review after completing the individual call reviews.

### Materials considered

The synthesis may incorporate:

- individual call observations;
- PHUB entries;
- OneNote analysis;
- KPI results;
- production data;
- prior coaching;
- leadership context;
- relevant communication;
- repeated or contrasting behavior across calls.

### Purpose

The synthesis determines:

- whether an individual occurrence is isolated or recurring;
- whether multiple calls support a pattern;
- what root cause appears most likely;
- what should be addressed in coaching;
- which secondary observations should be retained rather than surfaced immediately.

### Output

The broader analysis is communicated separately from the individual call reviews.

---

## Stage 13: Prepare the Coaching Conversation

The underlying call review and evidence analysis must be complete before live coaching occurs.

Preparation may include:

- selecting the focal behavior;
- reviewing relevant metrics;
- identifying a positive strength;
- documenting the call example or timestamp;
- selecting a key call segment;
- preparing facilitative questions;
- determining the desired execution;
- preparing a role-play scenario;
- anticipating the commitment and follow-up needed;
- retaining backup calls if necessary.

### PHUB submission timing

Two sequences are used:

#### Sequence A: Submission before coaching
1. Complete the review.
2. Prepare the PHUB entry.
3. Submit the record.
4. Conduct the coaching.

#### Sequence B: Submission after coaching
1. Complete the review.
2. Prepare the PHUB content.
3. Conduct the coaching.
4. Finalize and submit the record.

Submission timing depends on:

- CP preference;
- agent or leader relationship;
- whether agent input may affect the final commitment or follow-up language.

The review itself is completed before coaching regardless of submission timing.

---

## Stage 14: Deliver the Coaching

The normal live-coaching conversation follows this sequence.

### 14.1 Set the stage

- Establish the purpose.
- Provide an agenda or conversational roadmap.
- Frame the session around development and expected performance.

### 14.2 Identify the focal behavior

- State the behavior being coached.
- Keep the conversation focused on that behavior.

### 14.3 Review the numbers

- Present relevant KPI or production results.
- Use the numbers to establish why the behavior matters.
- Connect the behavior to the desired customer, performance, or business outcome.

### 14.4 Review the call evidence

- Discuss the relevant call.
- Play a key moment when useful.
- Provide enough context to make the behavioral evidence clear.

Playing the call is normal but not mandatory in every circumstance.

### 14.5 Ask the agent to self-diagnose

The CP uses facilitative questions to explore:

- what the agent heard;
- what the agent intended;
- what could have been done differently;
- what prevented execution;
- whether the agent recognizes the behavior gap;
- what successful execution would sound like.

The agent’s response provides new diagnostic evidence. The response may confirm, refine, or change the CP’s provisional conclusion.

### 14.6 Role-play the optimal behavior

- Define what successful execution should look and sound like.
- Practice the expected behavior or talk path.
- Provide feedback.
- Repeat as necessary to increase confidence and consistency.

Practice, Drill, Rehearse may be used to develop efficacy.

### 14.7 Establish the agent commitment

The commitment identifies:

- **What:** The behavior the agent will execute.
- **Why:** Why the behavior matters.
- **How:** What the behavior will sound like or what steps will be taken.
- **Measurement:** What evidence will demonstrate progress.
- **Timing:** When execution will begin and when it will be reviewed.
- **Obstacles:** What might prevent execution and how that obstacle will be addressed.

Attempt, ownership, and momentum are treated as important, rather than demanding flawless execution immediately.

### 14.8 Establish the CP or leader commitment

The CP or leader identifies how support and accountability will be provided.

Possible commitments include:

- reviewing subsequent calls;
- listening live;
- looking for the agreed behavior;
- providing feedback;
- recognizing progress;
- reconvening if execution remains inconsistent.

### 14.9 Schedule follow-up

The coaching concludes with an agreed:

- context for follow-up;
- timeframe;
- evidence source;
- behavior to be evaluated;
- likely next action based on the result.

### Feedback pattern

Discussion of unsuccessful execution is generally placed between:

1. positive observations or reinforcement;
2. direct discussion of the gap;
3. encouragement and confidence-building.

This is confirmed current practice, although its status as a formal requirement is unknown.

---

## Stage 15: Submit and Distribute the PHUB Record

Once submitted, the PHUB review is automatically:

- sent to the reviewer;
- sent to the agent;
- indexed to the division leader responsible for the team.

PHUB therefore serves as:

- the formal coaching record;
- the practical review scorecard;
- an automatic distribution mechanism;
- a leadership visibility mechanism.

The record may be submitted immediately before or after coaching, depending on the selected sequence.

---

## Stage 16: Track the Follow-Up Obligation

The follow-up commitment may be tracked through several mechanisms:

- PHUB;
- Outlook;
- OneNote;
- leadership direction;
- CP memory.

The agent is expected to schedule the follow-up in Outlook.

### Current-state risk

No single mechanism has been confirmed as the authoritative control ensuring that every follow-up is completed.

---

## Stage 17: Evaluate Follow-Up Execution

Follow-up evidence is gathered through:

- live listening; or
- recorded calls selected through logic similar to the original review.

### Live listening

Live listening is commonly used in the follow-up stage because it provides immediate evidence of whether the agreed behavior is being attempted.

### Recorded-call selection

When recorded calls are used, the CP applies similar criteria:

- relevant date range;
- relevant call type;
- opportunity to demonstrate the behavior;
- appropriate call direction;
- fair selection from the eligible population.

---

## Stage 18: Determine the Follow-Up Outcome

Immediate perfection is not the primary standard.

The CP evaluates:

- whether the agent attempted the agreed behavior;
- whether the effort was genuine;
- whether the agent used feedback;
- whether the agent remains coachable;
- whether execution is improving;
- whether successful moments can be reinforced;
- whether the behavior is becoming more consistent.

### Outcome A: Successful execution

The CP:

- recognizes the successful behavior;
- connects the result to the coaching;
- reinforces confidence;
- encourages continued execution;
- determines whether the behavior can close or needs additional validation.

### Outcome B: Attempted but inconsistent execution

The CP:

- recognizes the effort;
- identifies where execution broke down;
- provides further feedback;
- uses additional Practice, Drill, Rehearse as appropriate;
- clarifies the desired behavior;
- establishes another observation or follow-up cycle.

### Outcome C: No meaningful execution or repeated failure

The CP or leader may:

- determine what prevented execution;
- clarify whether the issue is ability, understanding, willingness, or accountability;
- recommit to the expected behavior;
- conduct further formal coaching;
- increase structure and observation;
- consider formal performance-management action when repeated offenses warrant it.

Formal coaching or a performance improvement plan may be used for repeated issues, subject to leadership guidance and the applicable performance-management process.

---

## Stage 19: Document the Follow-Up

Follow-up results are documented in:

- OneNote; and
- potentially PHUB, depending on leadership guidance.

### Current-state endpoint

A call-review coaching cycle appears to end when:

1. the behavior is demonstrated sufficiently and the coaching can close;
2. continued development is needed and another coaching cycle begins; or
3. repeated non-execution moves into formal accountability.

The precise closure standard remains unresolved.

# 4. Official vs. Informal Process

## Likely formal or authoritative elements

These elements are supported by system behavior or supplied enterprise guidance, although some still require source verification:

- PHUB contains standardized coaching fields.
- Submitted PHUB entries are automatically distributed to the reviewer and agent.
- PHUB entries are indexed to the applicable division leader.
- Coaching documentation includes the behavior, comments, commitments, and follow-up when applicable.
- The 4P framework supports preparation, partnership, practice, and planning.
- Coaching guidance calls for measurable and time-bound commitments.
- Follow-up should evaluate execution and determine next steps.

## Confirmed observed practice

- Direct leadership requests commonly initiate reviews.
- AWS Leader View is the preferred selection and listening system.
- Verint is used selectively for screen recordings, QA context, or previous reviews.
- One behavior anchors each individual review.
- Random sampling is the preferred selection standard after valid filters are applied.
- Calls are ideally recent, generally within one or two weeks.
- Inbound calls are normally favored.
- OneNote stores CP notes.
- Individual conclusions remain provisional until a summary review is completed.
- The call-review evidence is complete before the live coaching conversation.
- Coaching normally includes metrics, call evidence, facilitative questions, role-play, commitments, and follow-up.
- Attempt, effort, and coachability matter more than immediate perfection.
- Follow-up documentation may remain in OneNote or enter PHUB depending on leadership guidance.

## Personal or local methods

- Using Ctrl+F to remove clearly irrelevant interactions.
- Preparing backup calls.
- Keeping additional findings separate from the focal review.
- Retaining secondary observations in OneNote.
- Preparing several facilitative questions.
- Using successful calls as practice examples.
- Asking an agent to provide a successful and unsuccessful call.
- Emphasizing agent ownership and momentum.
- Balancing corrective feedback with positive reinforcement and encouragement.

## Material discrepancies

### One versus two behaviors
- Actual operating rule: one behavior focus per individual call review.
- Coaching guidance: one or two potential behaviors may be isolated during preparation.

Possible reconciliation: multiple behaviors may be considered during diagnosis, but only one becomes the formal focal behavior.

### Submission timing
- PHUB may be submitted before or after live coaching.
- The review analysis must be complete before coaching in either case.

### Follow-up documentation
- Guidance supports documentation and outcome tracking.
- Actual PHUB documentation depends on leadership direction.

### Email versus PHUB distribution
- PHUB automatically distributes submitted reviews.
- Individual reviews and summary analysis may also be emailed separately.
- The distinct purpose and authoritative status of each communication have not been established.

# 5. Observed Friction and Failure Points

## Fragmented information landscape

Relevant evidence is distributed across:

- AWS;
- Verint;
- Performance Hub;
- Client Connect;
- two KPI sites;
- Teams;
- OneNote;
- Outlook.

This creates manual context assembly and dependence on system knowledge.

## Variable call scorability

There is no stable minimum call length. Short calls can be unscorable or highly revealing depending on the behavior.

Possible consequences:

- inconsistent inclusion decisions;
- avoidable call switching;
- variation among CPs.

## Limited pre-review visibility

AWS does not normally reveal the sales result, final outcome, or meaningful behavior before review.

This supports fair sampling but may increase review time.

## Potential selection bias

Valid filters are necessary, but extensive filtering could unintentionally turn random sampling into confirmation-seeking.

## Richer evidence requires a slower system

Verint provides screen and QA evidence but adds friction. CPs must decide whether the additional evidence is worth the time.

## Behavior-focus boundary

The CP must preserve broader context without allowing secondary findings to dilute the focal behavior.

This boundary relies heavily on judgment.

## Root-cause uncertainty

Call audio can identify an execution gap without explaining:

- why it occurred;
- whether the agent recalled the training;
- whether confidence, judgment, process application, or willingness was the issue.

The agent coaching conversation is therefore part of diagnosis, not only delivery.

## Provisional evidence management

The CP must preserve individual findings without treating them as final until the complete review set has been synthesized.

## Multiple records and communications

A single review cycle can produce:

- OneNote notes;
- multiple PHUB entries;
- automatic PHUB messages;
- separate individual emails;
- a summary email;
- Outlook appointments;
- potential follow-up documentation.

This creates duplication and version-control risk.

## Variable PHUB submission timing

Submitting before coaching improves immediate record availability but may capture commitments before the agent has participated.

Submitting afterward may improve accuracy but delays the formal record.

## Follow-up tracking fragmentation

Follow-up may depend on:

- agent-created Outlook scheduling;
- PHUB;
- OneNote;
- leadership reminders;
- CP memory.

No single authoritative tracking control has been established.

## Unclear follow-up documentation standard

The result may be documented only in notes or may be entered in PHUB depending on leadership guidance.

## Subjective progress evaluation

Attempt and coachability matter, but CPs may interpret these differently.

## Escalation ambiguity

The threshold between:

- continued developmental coaching;
- repeated formal coaching;
- accountability action;
- a performance improvement plan

has not been defined in the collected workflow.

# 9. Transferable Structural Insights

## The workflow starts with recognition, not retrieval

A call review begins when a need is identified and scoped. Opening the call is several stages downstream.

## Selection is constrained sampling

Fair call selection generally means:

1. define a legitimate eligible population;
2. remove interactions that cannot answer the review question;
3. sample fairly from what remains.

Pure randomness without eligibility criteria can produce irrelevant evidence. Excessive filtering can produce confirmation bias.

## One behavior can require broad context

A focused review does not mean ignoring the rest of the interaction. Supporting context may explain whether the focal behavior was:

- available;
- properly set up;
- attempted;
- effective;
- affected by earlier actions.

## Observation and diagnosis are different

A call can show what occurred without proving why it occurred.

The workflow should preserve:

- observed action;
- suspected explanation;
- agent perspective;
- pattern across evidence;
- final diagnosis.

## Individual evidence should remain provisional

One interaction can justify feedback but may not justify a broad conclusion. Cross-example synthesis reduces overreaction to isolated events.

## The coaching conversation creates evidence

Agent self-assessment and role-play are not only delivery techniques. They help diagnose:

- understanding;
- recall;
- judgment;
- confidence;
- adaptability;
- willingness;
- ability to execute.

## Practice converts abstract feedback into observable behavior

“What should improve?” is weaker than “What should this look and sound like?” Role-play makes the commitment testable.

## Commitments require reciprocal ownership

A useful plan identifies both:

- what the employee will execute; and
- what the coach or leader will observe, provide, or do next.

## Follow-up is part of the original workflow

The call review does not end when documentation is submitted. It ends when execution is reassessed and the behavior is:

- closed;
- continued;
- redirected; or
- escalated.

## Progress is not identical to perfection

Early follow-up should distinguish among:

- no attempt;
- genuine but inconsistent attempt;
- improving execution;
- sustained execution.

This preserves accountability without treating development as a single binary test.

## Working notes and formal records serve different functions

A working repository can preserve context and provisional analysis. A formal system of record should contain the conclusion, commitment, visibility, and follow-up required for organizational accountability.

The workflow becomes vulnerable when the boundary between those records is unclear.

# 10. Condensed Workflow for V1 Development

```text
Recognize the review need
    ↓
Define scope and focal behavior
    ↓
Gather prior coaching, KPI, production, and situational context
    ↓
Choose AWS or Verint
    ↓
Define the eligible call population
    ↓
Randomly select a relevant call, unless a call is specified
    ↓
Review the broader interaction through one behavior focus
    ↓
Capture evidence and secondary observations in OneNote
    ↓
Create a provisional call-level finding
    ↓
Document one PHUB entry per call
    ↓
Repeat for the remaining calls
    ↓
Synthesize all calls and supporting evidence
    ↓
Prepare the coaching conversation
    ↓
Set the stage and connect results to the behavior
    ↓
Review call evidence and ask the agent to self-diagnose
    ↓
Practice the desired behavior
    ↓
Establish agent and leader commitments
    ↓
Set follow-up context and timeframe
    ↓
Submit or finalize PHUB documentation
    ↓
Track the follow-up through Outlook, PHUB, OneNote, or leadership
    ↓
Evaluate later execution through live or recorded evidence
    ↓
Reinforce, continue development, or escalate
    ↓
Document the outcome and close or restart the cycle
```

## V1 Baseline Conclusion

The current process is not simply **Prepare → Select → Review → Diagnose → Decide → Document → Deliver → Follow Up**. The reconstructed workflow includes three important additional distinctions:

1. **Eligibility definition before selection**, because a fair sample must still be relevant.
2. **Provisional call-level analysis followed by cross-call synthesis**, because isolated examples do not automatically establish root cause.
3. **Agent-assisted diagnosis during coaching**, because the call establishes behavioral evidence but may not explain why execution failed.

The most significant unresolved operational weaknesses are fragmented evidence sources, duplicated records and communications, inconsistent follow-up tracking, and an undefined closure or escalation threshold. These are preserved as current-state findings rather than prematurely redesigned solutions.
